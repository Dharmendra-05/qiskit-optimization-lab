\begin{abstract}
Combinatorial resource allocation under capital budget and risk constraints is a foundational dilemma across sustainable energy systems. This report chronicles the complete research, mathematical formulation, implementation, benchmarking, and critical assessment of the \textbf{Quantum Resource Optimizer}, developed for the Qiskit Fall Fest 2026 Track 2 Optimization hackathon. We map the constrained Markowitz mean-variance portfolio selection problem into a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) model with an exact quadratic budget penalty. Using modern Qiskit 1.0+ and 2.0+ primitives (\texttt{QAOAAnsatz}, \texttt{StatevectorEstimator}, and \texttt{StatevectorSampler}), we synthesize a diagonal Ising Cost Hamiltonian and execute the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA). We benchmark the quantum solver against exact brute-force search and classical greedy heuristics. Our empirical results demonstrate an Approximation Ratio of $1.0000$ recovering the global optimum across all depths, and show that increasing QAOA circuit depth from $p=1$ to $p=2$ increases the Feasibility Yield from $41.65\%$ to $77.00\%$. Finally, we provide a candid scientific assessment of physical NISQ hardware bottlenecks, including gate error accumulation, heavy-hex SWAP routing overhead, and barren plateaus.
\end{abstract}
