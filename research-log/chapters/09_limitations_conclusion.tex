\subsection{Candid Scientific Assessment of Limitations}
Maintaining scientific integrity requires stating what current quantum optimization results prove and what they do not prove:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No Demonstration of Quantum Advantage:} Solving an $N=5$ combinatorial problem classically via brute force requires only 0.8 milliseconds. Simulating the same problem quantumly on a classical computer requires 1.4 seconds. This demonstration proves algorithmic correctness and educational understanding, not quantum speedup.
    \item \textbf{Physical NISQ Noise:} On actual superconducting hardware, two-qubit CNOT gate error rates ($\sim 0.5\%-1.0\%$) and finite coherence times ($T_1, T_2 \sim 200\,\mu\text{s}$) degrade the fidelity of deep circuits ($p \ge 3$), washing out the probability peaks observed in noiseless statevector simulations.
    \item \textbf{SWAP Network Overhead on Heavy-Hex Lattices:} The dense covariance coupling between energy assets requires all-to-all qubit interactions. Physical IBM Quantum chips feature sparse heavy-hex connectivity, requiring the insertion of SWAP networks that multiply physical CNOT depth by $3\times$ to $5\times$.
    \item \textbf{Barren Plateaus in Variational Landscapes:} As problem instances scale ($N > 20$), gradients of the variational expectation value vanish exponentially in $N$, impeding gradient-free classical optimizers.
\end{itemize}
